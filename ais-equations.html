Integrity Protocol · Current implementation

Agent Integrity Score in functional form

AIS is a verification-tier-capped, ZK-boosted weighted geometric mean of four behavioral components aggregated over the Oracle's trailing reporting window (30 days by default).

Inputs

v · Performance variance

Average inconsistency across reported executions. Lower is better.

h · Human grounding

Average grounding index in the interval [0, 1]. Higher is better.

g · Contribution proxy

Summed hours-equivalent value derived from token volume. It is not independently verified GPU time.

p · Penalty ratio

Fraction of events flagged by policy evaluation, in the interval [0, 1]. Lower is better.

z · ZK evidence

A Boolean indicating whether at least one proof was verified during the reporting period.

t · Identity tier

The agent's effective verification tier, used to limit its attainable score.

How each input changes its component

These 2D transfer curves make the scoring behavior visible. Every vertical axis is a normalized component score from 0 to 1000.

Stability · E(v)

Variance produces a steep Gaussian decay.

Grounding · G(h)

Grounding maps linearly from 0 to 1.

Contribution · S(g)

Early contribution matters most; returns diminish logarithmically.

Compliance · C(p)

The score falls linearly as the flagged ratio rises.

Three-axis volume interpretation

Normalize e=E/1000, g=G/1000, and s=S/1000. The block's weighted side lengths are e^0.30, g^0.30, and s^0.20. Their product is the three-axis behavioral volume. Compliance then contracts that volume by c^0.20; verified ZK evidence multiplies it by 1.15.

Component functions

E(v)=1000e−1.5max(v,0)2
Entropy/stability: Gaussian decay penalizes erratic behavior.
G(h)=1000·clip(h,0,1)
Grounding: a direct linear mapping onto the 0–1000 scale.
S(g)=1000·minlog10(max(g,0)+1)3,1
Sacrifice/contribution: logarithmic returns, saturating around 1000 hours-equivalent.
C(p)=1000·1−clip(p,0,1)
Compliance: every increase in the flagged-event ratio reduces this component linearly.

Composite score

AISraw=B(z)·E0.30·G0.30·S0.20·C0.20
B(z) = 1.15 with verified ZK evidence; otherwise B(z) = 1.00.

Because this is a geometric mean, any component equal to zero makes the entire raw score zero. Strong performance on one axis cannot fully compensate for a missing axis.

Propagation rule

The Rust Oracle is the sole formula authority.

The middleware accepts the Oracle's geometric, tier-capped ais value and divides out only its reported ZK multiplier before pushing the base to ReputationRegistry. It does not reconstruct the mean from component fields. The contract can then apply its independently earned temporary 1.15× ZK boost.

This keeps formula and tier policy in one implementation while preserving the contract's separate proof-verification boundary.
